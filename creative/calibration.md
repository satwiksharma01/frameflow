# Calibration: what AI-made video always does

Your first instinct for a grade and a caption style is the category's; treat it as spent.

## Habits already spent

Each of these is what an AI-made video does by default. None is banned where a direction argues for it, but none may arrive by habit:

- centred captions, white with the active word in yellow, on every short, whether anyone speaks or not;
- a slow push-in on every still, and a zoom punch on every sentence;
- a whoosh or a whip on every cut;
- a teal-and-orange or a warm-film grade by default;
- stock B-roll under uplifting corporate music;
- neon gradient title cards with a glow;
- letterbox bars plus slow motion, offered as "cinematic";
- a synthetic voice over a music bed.

## Faces already spent

As caption and title faces, these read as the default, not as a choice: Montserrat, Bebas Neue, Anton, Poppins, Impact, Arial Black, Inter set as a title face, and the default caption faces of the short-form apps. A brand kit that names one of them is the creator's commitment and stands; a direction does not reach for them on its own.

## The test

If the edit could be guessed from the category alone, or from the category plus what it avoids, rework it.

Apply it to the lead before the hand is written, and again to the storyboard: name what an outfit reel, a pitch or an explainer always looks like, and check that this one could not be predicted from that, nor from simply doing its opposite.
