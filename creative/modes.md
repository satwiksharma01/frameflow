# Modes: what success is for the viewer

A video's mode is set in its brief (`mode`). The mode, not the kind of video, decides how far a direction may reach. An outfit reel is usually Show, a talking short Stop, a pitch Persuade, an explainer or a software demo Teach, a vlog Tell; the brief decides, not the label.

## Stop

**The viewer** is scrolling, thumb already moving.
**Success:** they stop, stay to the end, and watch it again.
**How far a world may reach:** all the way. Boldness is the job; a polite video is a skipped one.
**The first two seconds** carry everything: something moving or written on the very first frame, and a reason to see the third second.
**Worth asking:**
- What would make someone stop who has never heard of the creator?
- What is the one image that could stand for the whole video?

## Show

**The viewer** wants to look: at an outfit, a place, a product.
**Success:** they feel the thing, its texture, its fit, its light, and want it or want to be there.
**How far a world may reach:** far. The work leads, and a world may own the frame, as long as the thing shown is never hidden by it.
**The first two seconds** put the best of the thing on screen, not a title about it.
**Worth asking:**
- Which detail is the one a buyer or visitor would look for first?
- Where does this audience already look at things like this: a magazine, a shop floor, a feed?

## Persuade

**The viewer** must decide: invest, buy, sign up, back the idea.
**Success:** they believe one claim and act on it.
**How far a world may reach:** only as far as it serves clarity. The claim and its proof must be unmistakable; a world gives them a voice, never a disguise.
**The first two seconds** state or show the claim, or the problem it answers.
**Worth asking:**
- What is the one claim, and what is its proof on screen?
- Who is the sceptic in the room, and what would they need to see?

## Teach

**The viewer** has a question.
**Success:** they understand, and could repeat it to someone else.
**How far a world may reach:** a little. Structure and legibility come first; the world lives in details: type, a colour, how a step is marked. Atmosphere worlds are rarely right here.
**The first two seconds** name what will be learned, or show the result.
**Worth asking:**
- What does the viewer know already, and what is the one step that loses people?
- What should be on screen when the explanation is in words?

## Tell

**The viewer** follows a story.
**Success:** they are carried to the end.
**How far a world may reach:** through rhythm and sound more than graphics. Restraint on screen; the cut and the music do the work.
**The first two seconds** open in the middle of something: a moment, a line, a sound.
**Worth asking:**
- Where does the story turn, and what should the viewer feel there?
- What can be left unsaid, because the picture says it?
